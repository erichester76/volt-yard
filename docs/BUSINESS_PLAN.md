# Amped Up Network Internal Business Plan

## Purpose

This is an internal operating plan for building Amped Up Network into a trusted EV ownership network. It is not investor, franchise, legal, consumer, or partner-facing material. Figures are planning assumptions, not revenue claims.

## Working Brand

Use **Amped Up Network** as the working brand for this plan. It describes a network of EV owners, independent service partners, community experts, and ecosystem vendors without implying a physical garage chain, an exclusive territory, or a franchise.

**Amped Up Electric Garage** is the intended founding shop and first founding network partner, not the network's primary name. It gives the network a real operating reference point: the first site to complete the launch program, meet the certification evidence requirements, host events, and validate member services. It must be held to the same published standards, review process, match-routing rules, and escalation process as every other partner.

Keep the organizations commercially and operationally distinct. Amped Up Network is the independent platform and certification brand; Amped Up Electric Garage is an independent service provider within that network. If they share common ownership, disclose that relationship in directory profiles, member communication, public methodology, and any matching logic. Do not give the founding shop undisclosed preferential placement or exempt it from certification review.

Before public launch, complete trademark, domain, social-handle, state corporate-name, and category-conflict clearance. The repository and public application currently use **Volt Yard**; a brand migration requires a separate approved product, legal, design, domain, email, Stripe, and database-content plan. This document does not authorize a public rebrand.

## Thesis

Independent EV owners need trustworthy help between an observed issue and a successful repair. Independent shops need qualified EV demand, credible differentiation, and a way to build durable relationships with owners. Amped Up Network provides the connective layer:

```text
Issue and vehicle context -> trusted guidance -> qualified shop match -> repair outcome -> verified community intelligence
```

The community is the trust and data layer. Certification makes local supply dependable. Membership makes ownership support recurring. Managed services and qualified completed bookings create transaction revenue. The product remains EV-focused; it is not a generic mechanic directory.

## Operating Model

Amped Up Network is initially an independent network company, not a member-owned cooperative and not a franchise.

- Amped Up Network owns the software, brand, certification standard, member experience, and marketing assets.
- Shops, including Amped Up Electric Garage, remain independently owned and set their own repair pricing, staffing, methods, schedules, warranties, and business decisions.
- Shops pay for assessed launch services and ongoing network membership; certification is earned, monitored, and revocable.
- Customers pay for ownership support, trusted matching, service benefits, and community access.
- The platform earns from subscriptions, productized launch services, managed services, and completed matched work.
- An elected shop advisory council should influence standards, roadmap priorities, and regional programming without receiving ownership or control in the first phase.

### Legal Boundary

Do not characterize this as a franchise or sell it as one without franchise counsel. A trademark license, substantial control or assistance, and a required payment may create franchise obligations regardless of contract labels. State law can impose broader requirements. Counsel must review the partner agreement, certification rules, training, mark usage, fees, territory language, earnings statements, and sales process before launch.

Avoid the following until counsel approves a compliant structure:

- Exclusive territories or protected geographic monopolies.
- Mandatory repair pricing, mandatory parts sources, or control of ordinary shop operations.
- Earnings claims or promises of booking volume.
- Describing a fee as buying certification or guaranteed demand.

## Customer Value Proposition

### EV Owners

- A vehicle-aware record of issues, symptoms, prior service, and repair context.
- A path from uncertainty to the right level of help: self-service learning, community experience, paid expert input, certified shop match, or managed service.
- EV-specific shop capability data rather than generic star ratings.
- Transparent service terms and a documented escalation path when a partner experience goes wrong.
- Relevant local community, events, and partner offers.

### Shops

- Qualified local EV demand with case context, rather than low-intent directory leads.
- A credible earned certification and public capability profile.
- Training, readiness assessment, launch marketing, member acquisition support, and local events.
- Community reputation through verified expert participation.
- Issue and outcome intelligence that identifies common local/model-specific needs.
- A repeat-customer relationship that remains with the shop after the first match.

### Ecosystem Partners

- A targeted EV-owner audience and trusted, measurable distribution surface.
- Co-branded education, local events, product offers, and managed-service opportunities.
- Aggregated, non-identifying ownership and issue trends where permitted.

## Partner Program

### Partner States

| State | Qualification | Customer-facing promise | Commercial terms |

| Listed | Claimable profile and basic business verification. | Directory presence only. | Free or low-cost. |
| Verified Partner | Business, insurance, named staff, capabilities, service area, and baseline terms verified. | Verified information, not a quality claim. | Monthly membership. |

| Network Leader | Sustained Certified outcomes plus education, events, and constructive community participation. | Regional leader and event host. | Higher membership tier and co-marketing options. |

Certification is separate from payment. A shop can pay for a launch assessment and require remediation, defer certification, fail to qualify, or later lose certification.

### Certification Metrics

The first version should assess and then periodically revalidate:

- Business legitimacy, insurance, licenses, and warranty terms.
- High-voltage safety process, equipment, PPE, isolation procedures, and incident escalation.
- Named technician experience, EV training, and supported vehicle makes/models.
- Diagnostic equipment, software access, battery/thermal/high-voltage capability, and stated repair scope.
- Estimate clarity, communication, appointment reliability, and documented customer handoff.
- Verified post-service feedback, repeat issue rate, warranty handling, and serious-complaint review.
- Availability, match acceptance, response time, and capacity reliability.
- Accurate, disclosed community expertise and event/education contribution.

Do not publish a single opaque score. Publish what the shop can do, evidence-backed certifications, supported vehicles, warranty policy, and verified outcomes in plain language.

### Required Partner Launch Program

Every active paid partner completes a productized launch program before its public certified-network launch. This is onboarding and marketing work, not a purchase of certification.

Amped Up Electric Garage completes this program first as the founding-partner pilot. Record its costs, staff hours, conversion, exceptions, member feedback, and remediation needs so the launch program is priced and scoped from operating evidence before it is sold widely.

**Planning price:** $3,000-$6,000 one time, excluding venue, catering, paid-media, and other third-party event costs. A founding cohort can receive a limited discount in exchange for feedback, case studies, and permission to use approved launch assets.

Deliverables:

1. EV readiness assessment covering safety, tools, training, scope, customer experience, insurance, and warranty.
2. Written gap report and remediation/training plan.
3. Team training on member experience, issue-case context, estimate transparency, booking, escalation, and community conduct.
4. Complete directory profile: capability matrix, supported vehicles, service scope, photos/video, staff credentials, service terms, and booking/contact configuration.
5. A launch offer with capacity limits and written scope. Example: a complimentary, limited cooling-and-battery-system inspection. It must disclose exclusions, not represent a diagnosis, and not pressure members into unneeded work.
6. A co-hosted local launch event: EV clinic, technical session, cars-and-coffee, shop open house, or car show.
7. Co-branded email, social, community, and local press launch assets.
8. A 30/60/90-day performance review covering qualified requests, completed work, feedback, issue outcomes, response time, and member conversion.

## Community and Expert Strategy

Community is not an unmoderated marketing channel for shops. It produces trusted owner support and structured issue intelligence.

- Shops may earn verified expert identities after certification or separate qualification.
- Expert answers must disclose affiliation, distinguish education from diagnosis, and avoid off-platform solicitation.
- Shops cannot claim or answer their own open match requests in a way that bypasses neutral routing.
- Member questions, case pathways, accepted matches, completed work, and verified outcomes should improve the directory's matching and certification evidence.
- Reward useful participation with profile prominence, event eligibility, membership credits, or fixed expert fees; do not pay a percentage of work won from an answer.
- Maintain moderation, conflict-of-interest rules, escalation, and a process to correct unsafe or inaccurate content.

Existing product support: paid members can participate in a moderated community; Premium members can open paid expert opportunities; shop owners can claim eligible work; expert payouts are currently recorded manually.

## Service and Transaction Strategy

Use two deliberately different commercial paths. Do not pretend every repair is a managed service.

### 1. Directory Match / Generic Repair Referral

Use when the required work is uncertain, high-variance, diagnostic, repair-specific, or cannot be responsibly pre-priced by Amped Up Network.

- Amped Up Network captures vehicle and issue context, presents suitable partners, and tracks the referral.
- The shop quotes, contracts, bills, performs, and warranties work directly with the customer.
- Amped Up Network earns the shop's recurring membership and, after legal review, a fixed fee for a verified completed qualified booking.
- Planning completed-booking fee: $25-$75. Start fixed, not percentage-based, to reduce disputes and perverse incentives.
- No fee is due for a cancelled request, unmatched contact attempt, or work not attributable to the platform under documented rules.

### 2. Managed / Preset Services

Use only for bounded, repeatable services with predictable scope, compatible partner capacity, clear inclusions/exclusions, a known partner payout, and sufficient margin for customer support and exceptions.

Amped Up Network sells the service through the catalog, records a retail price and partner payout, receives payment, matches eligible partners, and retains the platform margin. The current application already supports this model with Stripe Checkout, stored price/payout/margin snapshots, qualified partner work offers, and manually recorded partner payouts.

Candidate managed services to validate:

| Service | Why it can be bounded | Product revenue model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EV cooling and battery-system inspection | Defined checklist and report, with clear limits. | Retail price less partner payout; optional member subsidy or launch promotion. |
| Pre-purchase EV inspection | Standardized inspection/report, vehicle-specific exclusions. | Retail price less inspector payout. |
| EV health baseline / ownership intake | Standardized vehicle, charging, tire, brake, thermal, and service-history review. | Retail price less partner payout; leads to maintenance and membership. |
| Seasonal EV readiness inspection | Repeatable checklist for tires, braking, HVAC, charging, fluids, and thermal system. | Retail price less partner payout; recurring seasonal campaign. |
| Charger installation assessment | Defined site/vehicle assessment, subject to electrical scope exclusions. | Retail price less installer payout; product or install referral revenue only with disclosed terms. |
| Road-trip / towing readiness review | Standard checklist; no guarantee of reliability. | Retail price less partner payout. |

Do not productize major battery repair, collision work, unexplained fault diagnosis, broad repair estimates, warranty work, or any service whose scope routinely changes before the network has enough operational data.

### Product and Vendor Revenue

Products must improve the member experience and never secretly influence directory ranking or certification.

- Curated catalog products paired with a managed service: chargers, charging accessories, tires, wipers, cabin filters, detailing/maintenance items, and safety accessories.
- Revenue options: wholesale margin, disclosed affiliate commission, sponsored member offer, or vendor-funded promotion.
- Maintain a product approval rubric: EV relevance, warranty/support quality, availability, returns, pricing integrity, and no unsafe claims.
- Clearly label affiliate/sponsored offers and keep editorial recommendations separate from commercial placement.
- Inventory-light is preferred at launch. Do not buy or warehouse inventory unless recurring demand, margin, fulfillment quality, and working-capital needs justify it.

## Vendor and Distribution Partnerships

### Automotive and EV Vendors

Targets include chargers/electrical installation, tires, parts, insurance, roadside, vehicle-history, inspection tooling, detailing/maintenance, and EV-focused warranty or protection providers.

Partnership forms:

- Member offer or affiliate arrangement.
- Curated product/service bundle.
- Vendor-funded managed-service promotion.
- Education content, technical session, or local event sponsor.
- B2B distribution to fleets, employers, lenders, insurers, warranty administrators, or dealer groups.

Requirements: disclosed commercial relationship, defined customer support owner, privacy/data-use terms, no pay-to-rank directory placement, and a measurable outcome.

### Avants and Community Partners

Avants is an adjacent enthusiast-community partner, not a repair-network replacement. Its public model includes membership tiers, local events, a national and regional partner-discount network, editorial content, and automotive-brand partnerships.

Proposed pilot:

1. Co-host a regional EV ownership technical session at a Certified Partner shop.
2. Give Avants members a limited Amped Up Network Member trial or a defined directory/issue-tracker benefit.
3. Offer an Avants-specific diagnostic/inspection promotion with a clear shop-funded or vendor-funded budget.
4. Track trial activation, appointment requests, completed work, attendance, member retention, and partner NPS for 90 days.

Do not bundle memberships permanently or represent either organization as endorsing the other until an executed agreement defines customer support, branding, data, benefit funding, and renewal treatment.

### Do We Need A Loan?

Not necessarily at launch. A conventional loan is a poor first source for unproven recurring revenue, event/marketing experimentation, and product development. Begin with the smallest capital source that matches the risk:

1. Customer-funded launch fees and annual shop/consumer subscriptions should fund market activation where possible.
2. Founder capital, revenue-based financing, or a small working-capital line can cover timing gaps after recurring revenue and collections are demonstrated.
3. Equity or strategic capital becomes more appropriate when expanding multiple markets, building a partner-success team, or funding a scalable B2B product.
4. Use debt only for predictable uses with clear repayment capacity, such as receivables/working capital or equipment that Amped Up Network owns and can secure. Do not borrow heavily to subsidize shop launches, speculative events, or inventory.

Before seeking a loan, build a 13-week cash forecast with actual timing for launch-fee collections, annual subscriptions, Stripe settlement, partner payouts, payroll/contractors, refunds, insurance, and event deposits. The threshold question is cash-flow coverage, not whether the annual projection is positive.

## First 12 Months

### Phase 1: Design the Market (0-60 Days)

- Select one EV-dense metro and one initial customer segment.
- Interview 15-25 shops and 30-50 EV owners; validate pain, willingness to pay, service demand, and event interest.
- Draft certification rubric, partner agreement, launch checklist, member promise, and escalation playbook.
- Obtain franchise, referral-fee, marketplace/payment, privacy, warranty, insurance, and local-regulatory legal review.
- Choose 3-5 bounded managed services for research, but launch no more than one or two.

### Phase 2: Founding Partner Cohort (60-150 Days)

- Recruit 6-10 founding shops; charge a discounted but real launch fee where possible.
- Run readiness assessments, training, profile production, and remediation.
- Launch 2-4 certified partners only when evidence supports the designation.
- Hold initial events and test the inspection/education offer with defined capacity.
- Enroll 100-250 founding members through owner communities, partner shops, and events.

### Phase 3: Prove Repeatability (150-365 Days)

- Operate the 30/60/90-day partner review process and remove/rework weak experiences quickly.
- Measure attributed match-to-completion conversion and customer outcomes.
- Introduce a single managed service only after scope, partner payout, support burden, and margin are proven.
- Test one Avants or comparable community pilot and one product/vendor offer.
- Decide whether to add shops in the same metro or replicate the playbook elsewhere only after partner and member retention meet thresholds.

## Operating Metrics

### Supply Quality

- Active Certified and Verified shops by market and capability.
- Certification pass, remediation, suspension, and renewal rates.
- Shop response time, match acceptance, completion, no-show, and capacity rates.
- Verified NPS, repeat issue rate, complaint/escalation rate, and warranty resolution time.

### Demand and Revenue

- Free-to-paid member conversion, annual member retention, and revenue per member.
- Partner launch revenue, monthly recurring partner revenue, and partner retention.
- Qualified request-to-booking and booking-to-completion conversion.
- Managed-service gross margin after all direct costs.
- Product/vendor revenue with explicit attribution and support cost.

### Community Trust

- Expert response time, acceptance rate, quality review, and correction rate.
- Moderation turnaround and safety/escalation incidents.
- Percentage of issue cases that reach a documented useful outcome.

## Decisions and Guardrails

- Build density in one market before expanding geography.
- Optimize for successful repair outcomes and repeat trust, not raw directory leads.
- Do not let paying vendors or shops alter certification, editorial guidance, or organic ranking.
- Do not market earnings, customer volume, repair savings, or certification outcomes that cannot be substantiated.
- Keep shops independent. Amped Up Network sets network standards, not shop operating control.
- Keep repair payments direct to the shop for generic referrals. Use managed payment only for carefully bounded preset services.
- Finance events and promotions with clear budgets and attribution. A free inspection is an acquisition cost, not a vague brand expense.
- Maintain a cash reserve before paying partners faster than customer payments settle or before carrying product inventory.
